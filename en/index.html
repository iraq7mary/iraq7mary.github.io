What a Unix timestamp is and how it converts

A Unix timestamp is the number of non-leap seconds since the UTC epoch, 1 January 1970 00:00:00, counting 86400 seconds per day. For example, 1000000000 is 2001-09-09 01:46:40 UTC. Note that a signed 32-bit timestamp overflows on 2038-01-19 03:14:07 UTC (the 2038 problem).

Frequently asked questions

How do I convert a Unix timestamp to a date?

Paste the timestamp into the box above and convert. 10 digits are seconds, 13 are milliseconds; the tool shows the matching time in UTC and zones like Beijing and US Eastern.

Which time zone is a timestamp in?

A Unix timestamp has no time zone - it is the number of seconds since 1970-01-01 00:00:00 UTC, a single global instant; you only pick a zone to display it as a wall-clock time.

Why does the customer time differ from my backend?

Usually a time-zone gap. Backends often use UTC or Beijing time, while the customer states their local time. Convert the same instant into both zones to align.

Does daylight saving affect conversion?

Yes. US Eastern, London and Sydney observe DST, so their gap from Beijing shifts by season. The tool uses the browser built-in IANA data to handle it automatically.

What is the 2038 problem?

A signed 32-bit timestamp maxes out at 2038-01-19 03:14:07 UTC and then overflows. Modern systems use 64-bit, so everyday support need not worry, but watch older system logs.

Does the tool upload my data?

No. All timestamp and time-zone conversion runs locally in your browser - nothing is uploaded or stored.

Can it convert millisecond timestamps?

Yes. 13 digits parse as milliseconds, 10 as seconds; you can also paste an ISO date (such as 2026-06-30T12:00:00Z) to get the timestamp back.

Quick real-world checks

  • An overseas customer asks when their order shipped: the backend stores a UTC timestamp; convert it to the customer zone and tell them their local time to avoid confusion.
